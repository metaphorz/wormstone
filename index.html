How this model works

The reference photograph guides the dense, varied openings and gray-beige finish. This is a procedural approximation, not a reconstruction from the photograph. The unseen interior is unknown; the deep channel layout is a modeling hypothesis. The original Random Curve Lab supplies a positive, closed Fourier radial profile. In 3D, that profile twists, swells, and drifts along a tube. Openings are distributed irregularly across the surface, and their channels extend deeply inward in different directions. A thin stone divider is retained where neighboring channels approach one another, preventing their voids from merging into large chambers. The surface is extracted with marching cubes. The sleeve view shows a single open, thick-walled tube, and slice guides expose its construction.

This is a geometric and material study inspired by porous carbonate rock, not a validated biological growth or beach erosion simulation. Units are arbitrary. Fine detail resolves small channels more accurately; high density needs more mesh detail to keep small openings visible. The realistic finishes use procedural mineral mottling and micrograin, with three lights, cavity darkening, and cast shadows. Wireframe displays the actual triangulated mesh without surface shading.

Built with locally bundled Three.js 0.183.2. No network access or API keys required. The original 2D lab also works standalone.
